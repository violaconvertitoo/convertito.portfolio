Purpose and research context

The Farm 5Gs Checklist is a voluntary whole-farm improvement instrument developed under FAO’s RENOFARM — Reduce the Need for Antimicrobials on Farms for Sustainable Agrifood Systems Transformation. It supports reflection on farm practices linked to animal health, production and the wider enabling environment. The Checklist operationalizes FAO's five interconnected pillars—Good Health Services, Good Production Practices, Good Alternatives, Good Incentives and Good Connections—through practical farm-level indicators, linking assessment and benchmarking to continuous improvement and advisory support. The field-testing survey assesses whether the questions and approach are clear, relevant and practical.

Digital instrument and scoring design

I programmed and refined the KoboToolbox/XLSForm instrument, including conditional display logic, validation, multilingual labels, weighted domain calculations and benchmarking. Its five sections cover farm information; approximately 100 yes/no practice questions; five-star benchmarking; action planning; and user feedback.

Weighted domain scoring

Domain score = round(domain weight × Yes responses ÷ applicable responses, 3)

Non-applicable answers are excluded from the number of applicable questions. A domain with no applicable questions contributes zero to the adjusted maximum. The overall percentage is the sum of earned domain points divided by the sum of adjusted maximum points, multiplied by 100.

Example: a 6-point domain with 3 Yes responses out of 4 applicable questions earns 4.5 / 6 points. The resulting weighted assessment informs the Checklist’s five-star benchmarking and improvement planning.

Field implementation and research outputs

The Farm 5Gs Checklist has been implemented in approximately40 countries, including Lesotho, Zambia and Zimbabwe, supporting its testing and adaptation across diverse livestock production contexts.

An initial mixed-methods pilot study, conducted between February and April 2026 using KoboToolbox, assessed the Checklist's usability, benchmarking capacity, score variability and preliminary construct validity. Quantitative analysis examined associations between weighted compliance scores and self-reported farm performance indicators, while qualitative thematic analysis explored users' implementation experiences and perceptions of the tool's usefulness.

Research manuscript (co-author):"From fragmented to holistic livestock advisory approaches: field-testing the Farm 5Gs Checklist to support whole-farm improvement and reduce the need for antimicrobials."

I contributed to the literature review, statistical analysis, development of figures and tables, and manuscript drafting.The manuscript is currently under internal review for journal submission.
