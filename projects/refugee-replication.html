RESEARCH PROJECT · 2024

Replication Analysis — Does Exposure to the Refugee Crisis Make Natives More Hostile?

Instrumental-variables replication and robustness extension · Master’s research project · 2024

RInstrumental variablesCausal inferenceMigration

Original study and research question

This project replicates and extends the instrumental-variables analysis of Hangartner et al. (2019), Does Exposure to the Refugee Crisis Make Natives More Hostile? The original study examines how exposure to refugee arrivals during the 2015 crisis relates to Greek island residents’ attitudes toward asylum seekers, immigrants and Muslims, policy preferences, and political engagement.

The core identification challenge is that refugee arrivals were not randomly distributed across islands; island characteristics may also be related to residents’ attitudes.

Identification strategy and replication approach

The study instruments refugee arrivals with log distance from the Greek island to the Turkish coast in a two-stage least squares (2SLS) framework. I replicated the IV approach in R and critically assessed instrument relevance, independence and the exclusion restriction. Geography alone cannot prove that distance affects attitudes exclusively through refugee arrivals.

Distance to Turkish coast (instrument) → Refugee arrivals (treatment) → Attitudes and policy preferences (outcomes)

The exclusion restriction requires no direct effect of the instrument on outcomes, conditional on the model’s assumptions.

Replication results and extension

The replication reproduced and examined the original paper’s outcome comparisons. I then extended the asylum-policy preference specification by adding respondents’ assessment of household finances, alongside demographic controls.

Takeaways

The extension shows a stable positive treatment coefficient after controlling for household-finance perceptions, while reinforcing the need to scrutinize IV assumptions and potential island-level confounding. The exercise strengthened my practical experience with 2SLS estimation, robustness analysis and transparent interpretation of causal research.
