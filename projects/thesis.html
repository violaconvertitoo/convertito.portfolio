RESEARCH PROJECT · MASTER’S THESIS

Empowering Women, Securing Food: How Patriarchal Norms and Marital Status Shape Women’s Access to Resources and Food Security in Syria

Master’s thesis · Quantitative research on gender, productive resources and household food security · 2025

Econometric analysisHousehold surveysGenderFood security

Research question and hypotheses

How are marital status, access to productive resources and women’s socio-demographic characteristics associated with food security among female-headed households in conflict-affected Syria? The study examines whether women’s household headship translates into meaningful resource access and improved welfare, considering patriarchal norms and constraints.

Data and empirical strategy

932female-headed households
2023baseline survey
3food-security outcomes

I cleaned and analysed household survey data and estimated multivariate pooled OLS models, with controls, to study associations rather than causal effects. The analysis considered marital status, education, land access and related household characteristics.

HDDSHousehold Dietary Diversity Score: food groups consumed over 24 hours.
FCSFood Consumption Score: weighted consumption frequency over seven days.
rCSIReduced Coping Strategy Index: coping behaviours during food shortages.
OLSMultivariate observational regression with covariates.

Key findings

  • Widowhood: associated with a 3.4-point lower Food Consumption Score (p = .004), suggesting greater vulnerability.
  • Land access: logged land size was positively associated with HDDS (+0.14; p = .017) and negatively associated with rCSI (−0.44; p = .020).
  • Education: not consistently associated with the measured food-security outcomes.
  • Interactions: the land ownership/size × widowhood interaction did not provide clear evidence of an additional effect at the 5% level.

Interpretation and limitations

Results underscore the role of resource access and marital-status-related vulnerability. Because the study uses observational baseline data and pooled OLS, findings should not be interpreted as identified causal effects. The analysis motivates further work on household resources, gender norms and food security in crisis settings.
